# Character voice

The author's favourite lines are ones only that character could say: 唐蕾's 还是打麻将好耍, 乐子's 醒了，不理，等它自己睡回去. The lines flagged worst were the writer's own words put in a character's mouth: 扇时间 from a 茶馆 boss, which Qwen called an author's 金句.

## The voice card

Keep one card per speaking character in the book's index doc.

- **Register:** Mandarin, how much 方言, or English and how it's rendered.
- **Rhythm:** sentence length and shape. 何蔓 is short and cutting and takes the last word. 冉峥 says 是 and speaks textbook English. 徐好翔 turns an old saying sideways, slowly.
- **What they compare things to:** 唐蕾 mahjong, Corinne counting pills, 邱野 training, the operators the 后屋 floor.
- **What they never say:** feelings outright; 冉峥 never asks why.
- **Tics:** one at most. Dialect comes from sichuanese-dialogue, net-speak from netspeak-dialogue.
- **Want:** what they're after in every scene; each line serves it.
- **Fixed lines:** catchphrases and running gags, kept word for word so callbacks stay exact.

## Checks for each chapter

1. **Cover test.** Hide the speaker tags. If two speakers could swap lines, rewrite one of them.
2. **金句 leak.** A line that sounds quotable, abstract or like narration (an aphorism, a balanced antithesis, a 比喻 from outside their world) gets a plain, concrete form the character would say, moves into the narration, or goes.
3. **Age and era.** Teenagers talk like teenagers of their year, not like the writer. Old men like 老蒲 use few words.
4. **Foreign speakers in Chinese.** Render them the way a good translator would: no 晓得 from Americans, and the spirit of their idiom kept.
5. **Over-answering.** A reply that answers in full is usually wrong. Prefer 不响, a deflection, or a question back.
6. **Cross-checks.** Every jab gets a comeback (roast-in-fiction), every dialect line is checked against the lexicon, and every net-speak line follows its skill.

## Record

Add new catchphrases and running gags to the card the day they're written.
