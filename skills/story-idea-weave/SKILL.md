# Weaving an idea into a story

Use this when the author drops in an idea mid-book: a one-line theme ("探讨性压抑"), a social type (a Chengdu Chad, an Amish girl on rumspringa, a rich woman who never spends a penny on men), an issue (surrogacy, 4B, radicalisation), a kink, or a world rule. The aim: the idea runs through the book as people and choices, never as a lecture, and nothing already written breaks.

## 1. Restate it as a question
- One line in a scratch note: what the author asked, at the size they asked. Don't widen it.
- Turn the idea into a question the cast can answer differently. 性压抑 becomes "what does each person do with desire they can't spend?" A thesis gets preached; a question gets dramatised.
- Ask the author only if the idea collides with a standing rule (children, real people, a book rule like "nobody cheats through a rented body"). Otherwise choose, and say what you chose.

## 2. Cast it on every side
- Pick 3 to 6 carriers who give different answers: at least one opposite answer, one that costs a lot, and one comic or cheap one. In 火候 the repression theme went to 乐子's rule (醒了，不理), 蒲青 who learned silence through a thin wall, 王鹏 who never goes upstairs, Lin and Kai's timed sex, 钟姐's prenups; against them the 一桌 four and 唐蕾.
- Existing characters first. Read the Map and pick thin or unsettled ones whose wants already lean that way. Add a new character only when nobody fits; a new one gets a full Map row (age; main story; in the air; politics · religion · AI · looks · temper) and a portrait drafted at first appearance.
- Nobody is the author's mouthpiece. Each carrier acts on their own interests, and the opposite view gets its strongest version.

## 3. Plug it into the engine
- Find where the book's premise bends the idea. If the sci-fi rule doesn't touch it, it's decoration. Rented bodies turn cheating into a question of whose hands; Cradle money turns a pregnancy into a sum Mei can work out; the Transfer Act turns abortion into "a child of mine out there calling someone else Mum".
- Tie it to the main story's clock (the ban, the dinner) so the idea has to be decided by a date.

## 4. Schedule seed, turn, payoff
- **Seed:** an object, a habit or one line, unexplained (Lin's wristband; the plaster on 邱野's arm, changed Mondays and Thursdays).
- **Turn:** a scene where the idea costs someone something.
- **Payoff:** a choice made on the page, not a realisation in narration.
- Spread these over chapters. Each chapter moves at least one carrier; one new seed per scene at most. Put the schedule in the Map's day table, so later chapters inherit it.

## 5. Show it, never state it
- No character delivers the theme as a speech; the narration never names it. The theme word lives in the Map only.
- Test each scene: delete the line that explains. If the scene still carries the idea, keep the deletion.
- Sensitive subjects (religion, radicalisation, abortion, 4B, incels): behaviour, not identity; research the realism (search; Qwen via qwen-consult for China); avoid the ending a reader expects (plot-unexpectedness can test it).

## 6. Retrofit what's written
- If earlier chapters need the seed, make the smallest edit: a line, an object, a gesture. Keep old/new pairs in an edit script, apply them to the local draft and the doc together, and verify the publish (block hashes).
- Run slowfire-zh-check on every touched chapter.

## 7. Record and report
- The Map: a dated "Decided" line (idea, carriers, schedule), new character rows, and any new canon fact. The book's index doc: one line per decision.
- Tell the author in a few lines who carries the idea, where it first shows, and anything you chose for them.
