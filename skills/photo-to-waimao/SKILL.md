# Photo to 外貌描写

Use this skill when the author sends a photo and asks for an appearance description, a 外貌 or a 肖像 of the person or people in it. The output is Chinese prose in the manner of the portraits in 《封火》 and 《火候》. It assigns no name.

## Hard rules

- Describe only what is visible. Never identify the person, guess who they are, or hint that they are well known. Treat everyone as a stranger.
- Sometimes a look is recognisable as a whole: a signature combination of hair, glasses and clothing. In that case change one or two details (colour of a jacket, a habit, an accessory) and say so in one line after the portrait, without saying why or who.
- No names. Use 她 or 他. With several people, write one paragraph each, labelled by position (左, 中, 右) or by a plain marker such as "穿白大衣的".
- Never sexualise. If anyone appears to be under 18, keep that portrait short and plain, with no attention to attractiveness.
- No racial or ethnic stereotypes, and no food metaphors for skin. Describe skin tone, features and hair concretely.
- Draw plain, heavy and ageing bodies exactly as carefully as beautiful ones.

## How to write it

1. **Observer.** If the author names whose eyes we see through (a rival, a mother, a lover, a stalker), write from there and let what they notice reveal them. If not, use a neutral close-third observer.
2. **Order, roughly:**
   - an age impression, as a judgment ("五十五了，看上去四十出头") or an age the author gives;
   - build and height;
   - the face: shape, skin, brows, eyes, nose, mouth and teeth when smiling;
   - hair;
   - hands;
   - clothes and jewellery, and how they sit on the body;
   - posture or gesture.
   Skip what the photo doesn't show; never invent a whole body from a face.
3. **One cutting detail, at the end.** One observed habit or inference that cuts against the praise or says what nobody says. It should be an action or an image, not a remark. Examples:
   - a hand always pressed on the knee, "像按着一沓钱";
   - a plaster changed on fixed days at the same spot;
   - "只有有房的人，才笑得这么不着急".
   The detail may lean on context the author gives (season, city, a character's situation). When the clothes are at odds with the weather or the place, that tension is often the detail.
4. **Length:** 150–250 汉字 per person.

## Style

- Plain modern Chinese in the 新写实 manner: specifics, numbers, prices, habits. Use short sentences sometimes, but not all the time.
- Use 成语 and 俗语 only where they fit naturally. No stacked adjectives and no long attributive chains.
- Check for 翻译腔 before handing over: "是……的一声", "……的那种……" overused, English word order, abstract nouns where a verb would do.
- If the author gives a dialect setting (such as Chengdu), the narration stays standard Chinese. Only quoted speech carries dialect.

## Delivering

Put each portrait in a blockquote. After it, add at most one line: which detail was changed for recognisability, if any, and an offer to rewrite it through a different observer. If the author then assigns a character, rewrite it through that character's point of view and save it where the novel's notes keep portraits.

## Examples (names removed)

> 她五十五了，看上去四十出头，这是花了钱的。头发黑得一根白的都没有，长长地披到背上，三天去店里吹一回。脸白，白得匀，下巴尖，眉毛细细两道，是纹过的。笑起来眼睛先弯，弯成两道月牙，露一排小白牙。耳朵上坠着两串水钻耳环，一偏头就晃。二月湿冷钻骨头，她穿一件奶白色的羊绒长大衣，底下光着两条腿，坐下来跷起二郎腿，大衣从膝盖那儿滑开。她一只手始终按在膝盖上，按着大衣的下摆，按得很稳，像按着一沓钱。

> 他六十出头，头发全白了，往后梳，梳出一道一道的齿印，看得出是出门前对着镜子梳的。一副细边眼镜，镜片擦得没有一个指印。脸方，颧骨高，一笑满脸的褶子一齐往眼角跑。一件深棕色的皮夹克，拉链拉到一半，里头的黑圆领衫规规矩矩扎进裤腰。两只手交叠着搭在膝盖上，指节粗，指甲剪得秃秃的。他笑得一点不着急，只有家里有房、手里有余的人，才这么笑。

> 她三十五六，个子不高不矮，栗色的头发天生是卷的，卷到肩膀，发梢晒浅了一截。脸窄，鼻子小，笑起来露一排整齐的牙，两边颧骨一下子鼓起来，眼角挤出细纹。一件藏青色的针织打底衫，领口三颗扣子扣到第二颗，袖子推到小臂，手腕上一根细细的金链子，别的首饰一样不戴。她对着镜头笑的那个笑，对开门的人是这样，对关门的人大概也是这样。
